Part 107 test FAQ

Plain answers about becoming a certificated remote pilot. Each answer links to the FAA source it comes from. Checked 11 November 2026.

How do I get a Part 107 remote pilot certificate?

Get an FAA Tracking Number by creating a profile in IACRA, schedule and pass the Unmanned Aircraft General – Small (UAG) knowledge test at an FAA-approved Knowledge Testing Center, then apply on FAA Form 8710-13 in IACRA. After a TSA background check you can print a temporary certificate, and the permanent one arrives by mail.

The test can take up to 48 hours to appear in IACRA, so you may not be able to apply on the day you pass. FAA: Become a Certificated Remote Pilot

How old do I have to be?

At least 16. You also need to be able to read, speak, write and understand English, and be in a physical and mental condition to fly a drone safely. 14 CFR 107.61

Where do I take the Part 107 test?

At an FAA-approved Knowledge Testing Center, by appointment. Bring a government-issued photo ID, and get your FAA Tracking Number in IACRA before you register. FAA: Become a Certificated Remote Pilot

What's on the Part 107 test?

The FAA lists regulations, airspace, aviation weather, loading and performance, emergency procedures, crew resource management, radio communication, the effects of drugs and alcohol, decision-making, airport operations, maintenance and preflight inspection, and night operations. Under aviation weather, the FAA's Remote Pilot Study Guide teaches reading METAR reports and TAF forecasts.

The full list of what's tested is in the FAA's topic areas and the Remote Pilot Airman Certification Standards. To practise reading weather reports, try the METAR & TAF decoder.

What happens if I fail?

You can retake the test after 14 calendar days. That waiting period is set by 14 CFR 107.71.

How do I keep my certificate current?

Complete the FAA's free online recurrent training every 24 calendar months. Without it, you may not use your certificate's privileges, so you can't act as remote pilot in command.

The course is Part 107 Small UAS Recurrent (ALC-677), on the FAA Safety Team website. 14 CFR 107.65 · FAA: Keep your certificate current

I already have a Part 61 pilot certificate. Do I need the test?

No. With a current flight review, you take the free online Part 107 Small UAS Initial course (ALC-451) on the FAA Safety Team website, apply in IACRA, and have your identity validated by a FSDO, a designated pilot examiner, an airman certification representative or a flight instructor. FAA: Existing Part 61 certificate holders

Can I fly at night under Part 107?

Yes, if you passed the knowledge test or training after April 6, 2021, and the drone has lit anti-collision lighting visible for at least 3 statute miles. The same lighting is required during civil twilight. 14 CFR 107.29
