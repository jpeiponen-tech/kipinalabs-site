How a METAR reads

The groups always come in the same order, so once you know the order, every report reads the same way.

  1. KMDWStation. The airport's four-letter ICAO code.
  2. 111953ZTime. Day of the month, then hours and minutes in UTC (Zulu).
  3. 27015G25KTWind. Direction it blows from, in degrees true, then speed and any gusts, in knots.
  4. 1 1/2SMVisibility. In statute miles. P6SM means more than 6.
  5. -RA BRWeather. Intensity, then a descriptor and the phenomenon: light rain, and mist.
  6. BKN015Clouds. Coverage and height above ground in hundreds of feet. The lowest broken or overcast layer is the ceiling.
  7. M02/M04Temperature and dew point. In °C; M means minus. A small spread means fog or low clouds are likely.
  8. A2992Altimeter setting. In inches of mercury: 29.92.
  9. RMKRemarks. Extra details, such as the station type or exact temperature.

About Sourced 107

Part 107 weather minimums

  • At least 3 statute miles of flight visibility from where you control the drone
  • At least 500 ft below and 2,000 ft horizontally from clouds
  • At or below 400 ft above ground, unless within 400 ft of a structure

14 CFR 107.51

Questions

What's the difference between a METAR and a TAF?

A METAR reports the weather observed at an airport, usually once an hour; a SPECI is an extra report when conditions change. A TAF is a forecast for the area within 5 statute miles of the airport, usually covering 24 or 30 hours.

What weather does Part 107 require?

Under 14 CFR 107.51, flight visibility from where you control the drone must be at least 3 statute miles, and the drone must stay at least 500 feet below and 2,000 feet horizontally from clouds. It must also stay at or below 400 feet above ground level, unless it's within a 400-foot radius of a structure.

Are cloud heights in a METAR above sea level?

No. Cloud heights in a METAR and a TAF are above ground level at the station, in hundreds of feet: BKN015 means broken clouds at 1,500 feet above the airport.

Where do I get current reports?

The FAA's Aviation Weather Center publishes them at aviationweather.gov. Paste one here to read it.
